Range & damage

Inside 110 m: 3 damage.
110–189 m: 2 damage.
190–280 m: 1 damage.

Altitude

Low, mid, or high. Climb and dive change one level. You must match the target’s level to fire.

Energy

Cruise, brake, and dive restore energy. Hard turns and climbing spend it. Energy also affects speed and how far you travel: higher energy usually means more distance is available. Aircraft, throttle, and turning change the actual displacement; check the move preview before committing.

Reversals

Show reversals adds tight 150° turns. They cost 2 energy. A diving reversal turns 180° and drops one level.

Aircraft, momentum, and next-turn previews

Lark: slower, tighter turns, quick acceleration.
Kestrel: balanced speed and handling.
Swift: fast, wider turns, gradual acceleration.
With Momentum rules, a hard bank blocks a hard turn the other way until you level out. Cruise always remains an option. Boost and brake adjust speed in stages. The gold path is your selected move; the faint teal paths show your available following-turn maneuvers. Neither preview reveals the rival’s choice.

Fixed or variable gunnery?

Fixed: a valid firing solution always deals the listed 1–3 damage; aim within the firing cone does not change that amount.
Variable: each of those 1–3 projectiles makes a separate hit check. Centered, close shots have a better chance. A displayed 70% means a 70% chance per projectile, not guaranteed damage or a promise about the rival’s next position. Missed bursts still spend ammunition. Both aircraft use the same rules.

Which difficulty should I choose?

Intro: learn three direction buttons with automatic hints. Land three hits to win.
Easy: the same three buttons, a rival that reacts, and hints only when requested. Reduce its 8 hull to zero. Your plane has 10 hull.
Normal: the rival weighs several possible moves.
Hard: the rival also watches your recent patterns and considers a second-turn response.
Normal and Hard keep the full flying rules. Intro and Easy handle speed and altitude automatically. Select a level, then Start flight to begin a new sortie.

How does a flight end?

Dogfight: reduce the rival’s 10 hull points to zero.
First blood: be first to deal 3 total damage. Reaching it together is a draw.
Break contact: reach the marked exit lane while opening at least 250 m of separation from the pursuing rival. Bringing it down also wins.
Both down together, 40 turns, or both out of ammunition means a draw.

What do the stars mean?

Earn one star for a win, another for finishing with at least 7 hull, and another for winning within 8 turns. Practice never changes your record.

Three useful habits
Bank toward the rival before chasing it. Check the following-turn paths before committing to a tight bank.
Use a reversal after passing each other.
Watch the rival’s firing line as well as your own.

Saving & replay

The status line tells you whether this browser saved the flight. Older saves retain Classic flight and fixed gunnery; start a new sortie to use the new rules. Reopen the same file in the same browser to continue. Replay spends no ammunition and awards no extra points. Flight recorder can play any completed turn or the whole recording, and export/import a JSON log. Imported recordings are read-only. The current rival’s pending move is omitted from exported logs. A hosted version is needed for convenient iPhone Safari access.

Design inspiration

This is an independent tribute to Alfred Leonardi and Douglas Kaufmann, the creators of Ace of Aces, and Nova Game Designs, the publisher behind the series. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by them. The two-book encounter and simultaneous maneuver idea in Ace of Aces are the primary inspiration. Other influences include simultaneous maneuver choices in Wings of Glory and the readable tactics and mission variety discussed in this Ace Patrol review.
